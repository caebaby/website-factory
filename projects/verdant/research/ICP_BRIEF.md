## 1. ICP Snapshot

**Who:** Founders (usually founder-CEO or founder + head of brand, pre-Series-B) of sustainable
CPG/DTC startups — food, personal care, home goods, apparel. Product ethics are real: they've done
the LCA work, sourced the better material, paid more for the certification. The brand has not
caught up to the product.

**Situation:** They're past the "just get something live" stage. They have traction, maybe a raise,
maybe retail conversations starting — and the identity they launched with (often a founder's Canva
deck or a cheap freelance logo) now actively undersells the company in rooms where it matters:
retail buyer meetings, investor decks, DTC shelf-test against category leaders.

**Dominant awareness level:** **Solution-aware, sliding toward product-aware.** They already know
"we need a real brand" — that's not news to them. What they haven't resolved is *which kind* of
agency solves it, because their pattern-match for "sustainability branding" and their pattern-match
for "an agency that will actually grow revenue" have never been the same vendor. That unresolved
tension is the sales conversation.

**Sophistication stage:** High. These are savvy operators who've been pitched by five branding
studios already. Generic sustainability-agency claims ("purpose-driven," "authentic storytelling")
are noise to them — they've heard it from every studio in the category. Differentiation has to be
mechanism-level (a specific process, a specific proof point), not adjective-level.

## 3. Pain Hierarchy

1. **The brand actively depresses the price they can charge.** (VOC-04, VOC-07, VOC-18) — Felt
   cost: real margin left on the table on every unit sold, every wholesale negotiation lost to a
   worse product with a better-looking brand.
2. **Visual sameness makes them indistinguishable from greenwashers.** (VOC-01, VOC-02, VOC-03,
   VOC-13, VOC-16, VOC-20) — Felt cost: the exact consumers who'd pay a premium for genuine
   sustainability write them off in a 2-second shelf/scroll glance, because the visual language
   they share with actual greenwashers (leaf logos, kraft, sage) triggers the skepticism reflex.
3. **No agency they've talked to solves both halves of the problem.** (VOC-10, VOC-11, VOC-12,
   VOC-19) — Felt cost: they either hire a "purpose brand" studio that produces something soft and
   unconvertible, or a growth/CRO shop that produces something generic and sustainability-illiterate
   — and they've likely already burned budget on one or both.
4. **The greenwashing accusation risk is asymmetric and severe.** (VOC-05, VOC-09, VOC-20) —
   Felt cost: this isn't just "meh branding," it's reputational and regulatory exposure — Gen Z
   calls it out publicly, regulators can fine up to 10% of global turnover, and once tagged a
   greenwasher, 77% of that consumer base stops buying entirely.
5. **The audience is actively, behaviorally scrutinizing them, not passively receiving a message.**
   (VOC-06, VOC-08, VOC-15) — Felt cost: this ICP's buyers already changed shopping habits over
   sustainability; a brand that reads as costume rather than proof is being *actively* discounted,
   not just failing to stand out.

## 6. Trust Signals

**What earns trust with this ICP:**
- Category exclusivity, stated plainly (Verdant works *only* with sustainable/climate-conscious
  startups — INTAKE.md) — signals fluency without having to claim it adjectivally.
- Insider vocabulary used correctly and sparingly: B-Corp, LCA, "kraft-paper trap," CPG shelf test
  (INTAKE.md) — proves category literacy in a way generic "purpose-driven" language cannot.
- Visible mechanism over adjective: a concrete before/after (the transformation-reveal signature
  moment, INTAKE.md) lets the founder *see* the proof rather than take a claim on faith — this
  directly answers VOC-01/VOC-03's "sustainability claims without meaning" fatigue.
- Real texture/material photography over stock botanical imagery (INTAKE.md visual-world spec) —
  because stock leaves/globes/seedlings are now themselves a greenwashing tell (VOC-13).
- A no-commitment diagnostic entry point (free Brand Assessment) — de-risks a category where
  founders have likely been burned by a prior branding spend.

**What backfires:**
- Any claim-heavy copy without visible proof — this ICP is the *most* qualified audience to detect
  and penalize overclaiming (VOC-01, VOC-05, VOC-09).
- Leaning on the same eco-visual code Verdant is positioned against (leaf logos, kraft paper, sage
  green, stock imagery) anywhere in Verdant's own site — would be a self-defeating category
  greenwash.
- Vague "authentic/purpose-driven storytelling" language — this is exactly the noise VOC-01/VOC-12
  say the ICP has learned to filter out.
- Fabricated or vague stats ("40+ sustainable brands") without illustrative labeling — INTAKE.md
  explicitly flags this must render as clearly illustrative or be replaced with capability claims.

## 8. Insider-Language Glossary + Kill List

**Insider terms (use correctly, sparingly — signals fluency):**
- Greenwashing
- B-Corp (certification)
- LCA (Life Cycle Assessment)
- "Kraft-paper trap" (Verdant's own coined framing — INTAKE.md; own it, don't over-explain it)
- Conscious consumer
- Climate claims (as a category of substantiation risk, not a vague virtue)
- CPG shelf test
- Category convention / visual code (design-fluent framing of "everyone looks the same")

**Kill list (marks an outsider / triggers eye-roll, per VOC-01, VOC-05, VOC-12):**
- "Purpose-driven" (unless immediately backed by a specific mechanism)
- "Authentic storytelling"
- "Eco-friendly" / "green" / "clean" used as unsubstantiated adjectives (VOC-01, VOC-02)
- "Sustainable" as a bare claim with no proof attached
- "Make an impact" / "do good" without a named, specific outcome
- Any copy that could be mistaken for stock sustainability-agency boilerplate — this ICP has heard
  it all already (VOC-12)
